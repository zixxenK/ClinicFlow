# **Team Expectations** 

 

## **Work Quality** 

 

1. **Project standards** (What is a realistic level of quality for team presentations, collaborative writing, individual research, preparation of drafts, peer reviews, etc.?): 

We will complete the assigned work minimum two days before the final deadline so that the team has one full day to review, provide feedback, and make necessary revisions. Team presentations and collaborative writing should be clear, organized, accurate, and consistent with the project requirements. Individual research should use reliable sources and be properly cited, with information checked for accuracy before being included in the final work. Drafts should be complete enough for meaningful peer review, and each member is expected to review other members' work and provide constructive feedback before final submission.


2. **Strategies** to fulfill these standards: 

 

We will break larger assignments into smaller tasks and assign each task to a specific member through the Trello board. Each member will complete their assigned work at least two days before the final deadline whenever possible. This allows the team to review, proofread, and make necessary changes at least one day before submission. Research will be supported by reliable and properly cited sources, and members will communicate any uncertainty or missing information to the group rather than leaving incomplete work for the rest of the team. The Quality Controller will review the final work for consistency, formatting, accuracy, and completeness before submission.

 

## **Team Participation** 

1. Strategies to ensure cooperation and equal distribution of tasks: 

 

Tasks will be divided based on workload and tracked through Trello. Each member will have clear responsibilities and deadlines. The weekly role rotation will ensure responsibilities are shared throughout the project. Tasks will be redistributed if one member has significantly more work.

 

2. Strategies for encouraging/including ideas from all team members (team maintenance): 

 

We will have an opportunity to contribute during meetings. We will listen to and consider everyone's ideas before reaching a consensus. Those who have not contributed will be given an opportunity to share their input.

 

3. Strategies for keeping on task (task maintenance): 

 

The Project Manager will keep meetings focused on the agenda, deadlines, and outstanding tasks. Trello will track assignments and progress. Before each meeting ends, the team will confirm what needs to be completed, who is responsible, and when it is due.

 

4. Preferences for leadership (informal, formal, individual, shared): 

 

Leadership will be shared through the weekly role rotation. The Project Manager will organize meetings and track progress, while major decisions will be made through consensus. All members remain responsible for the project.

 

## **Personal Accountability** 

 

1. Expected individual attendance, punctuality, and participation at all team meetings: 

We are expected to attend Thursday meetings at 1:15pm in the STM lobby outside the Makerspace and arrive prepared to participate. If unable to attend, members must notify the group chat as early as possible and remain responsible for their assigned work.

 

2. Expected level of responsibility for fulfilling team assignments, timelines, and deadlines: 

Members are responsible for completing their assigned work by the agreed deadline and keeping Trello updated. If a deadline cannot be met, the member must notify the team 24 hours before the deadline.

 

3. Expected level of communication with other team members: 

 

 Members are expected to regularly check the group chat and communicate project updates, problems, availability, and requests for help. Important decisions should be communicated to the entire team and posted on trello.

 

4. Expected level of commitment to team decisions and tasks: 

 

Once a decision is reached through consensus, members are expected to support it and complete their assigned responsibilities. If new information requires a change, the decision will be discussed with the entire team.

What Clinic Flow is supposed to do

For patients:

Find clinics, potentially by location

See which clinics are accepting new patients

Book appointments

Manage/cancel appointments

Get automated reminders

See waitlist status

Potentially access appointments with their family doctor

Support people without an Ontario health card, such as newcomers/international students

Eventually support English/French

Have accessible options for users with disabilities

For clinic staff:

Manage appointments and schedules

Manage patient bookings

Manage waitlists

See clinic/provider availability

Handle cancellations and no-shows

Manage multiple clinics/locations

Have a dashboard showing things like:

Appointment volume

Volume by provider

Cancellation rate

No-show rate

Average waiting time

Waitlist fulfillment speed

Provider utilization

Overall facility utilization

<a id="empathy-process"></a>

   1. ## **Empathy process**

Team Equilibrium started the empathy process by reviewing the ClinicFlow project brief and client Q\&A to identify the main groups affected by the appointment system. The team categorized these users into patients, clinic staff, and clinic administrators. Doctors are also affected by scheduling, but the project scope is strictly limited to administrative tasks, not replacing existing clinical systems.  
For patients, the system needs to handle finding clinics, checking if they are accepting new patients, and booking appointments. The client Q\&A pointed out several accessibility factors to consider, including support for non-OHIP patients, different levels of digital literacy, and bilingual or low-vision interfaces. These requirements led the team to focus on simple scheduling, clear waitlist rules, mobile access, and data privacy.  
To test these assumptions against real user behaviour, the team deployed a questionnaire. Instead of guessing where the bottlenecks are, the questionnaire asks respondents about how often they book, what interface issues they face, and what personal information they are comfortable sharing. This ensures the design targets confirmed functional problems rather than assumed ones.  
Front-desk staff handle daily patient flow and need tools for scheduling, managing waitlists, and checking provider availability. Since cancellations and no-shows lower clinic efficiency, the system needs to reduce manual work using automated notifications and clear availability tracking. The client also specified that clinic setup must take less than an hour and require no manual data re-entry, meaning the software needs to integrate into existing workflows easily.  
Clinic owners and administrators need summary reports to track performance without having to look at every single appointment. Important metrics include appointment volume, cancellation and no-show rates, waitlist speed, and provider utilization.  
Across all user groups, data privacy is the main technical constraint. ClinicFlow is an administrative tool, not an Electronic Medical Record (EMR). It must only collect the minimum data needed for booking, protected by standard authentication, encryption, and access controls.  
The initial client Q\&A raised several assumptions that still need field validation, such as how to handle non-OHIP patients and which accessibility features to prioritize. The next step in the empathy process is to combine the questionnaire results with direct interviews, including patients who need digital assistance and clinic staff. By comparing the client's intended workflow to real user feedback, the team will establish the final technical requirements for the ClinicFlow design.

The main things I noticed were limited appointment availability, long wait times, and having to call clinics for simple changes. ClinicFlow is divided into five subsystems with separate responsibilities. They communicate through a shared backend and database. Patient Appointment Management handles booking actions; Clinic Finder provides clinic information; Database and Data Management stores required records and controls access; Accessibility Settings stores and applies user preferences; and Notification System delivers reminders and updates. The notification subsystem sends messages based on appointment or clinic events but does not create or change appointments itself.

# **5**        **Subsystem Concepts**

## **5.1** **Cameron Kibble Concepts \- Database and Data Management Subsystem** 

This subsystem concept implements the core backend logic, secure data storage, and authentication workflows required for ClinicFlow. It is structured into three components:

1. Secure client server communication and authentication layer   
   1. Transport security: all data transfers from patient mobile app communicates via HTTPS with transport layer security (TLS) to prevent interception  
   2. Authentication and authorization: using an API authentication layer with an auth token issuer. User credentials are protected using hashed/encrypted passwords, secure cookies (SameSite=strict), and support for OAuth2 with OpenID Connect or similar third party login integration.  
   3. API token workflow: when a user signs up, a secure cryptographic token is generated.  
      i.e. a 32 byte random hex string \[crypto.randomBytes(32).toString(‘hex’)\]. The token is then stored in the database, displayed to the user once in a hidden fashion with options to regenerate if exposed. This API token is then required for all data transmission and retrieval requests.   
2. ~~PostgreSQL~~ firebase/superbase database: the backend relies on a structured postgreSQL database containing 5 core tables to manage operations   
   1. Patients: stores unique patient identifiers and profile details (PatientID, First Name, Last Name, DOB, Contact, RegistrationDate)  
   2. Staff: Tracks clinic personnel details and scheduling constraints (StaffID, First Name, Last Name, Role, Availability)  
   3. Clinics: Maintains facility metadata (ClinicID, Name, Address, Contact)  
   4. Appointments: Links patient, staff, and clinics to manage bookings (AppointmentID, PatientID, StaffID, ClinicID, Time, Date, Status)  
   5. Audit logs: Fulfills security and tracking requirements by recording system actions (LogID, ActionType, Timestamp, Details)  
3. App server management:   
   1. An application server processes core logic, routing requests between the database and the mobile interface to handle appointment creations, modifications, notifications, and administrative management seamlessly.

1. # **Introduction**

Patients need a clear way to find clinics, compare appointment options, and book a suitable time. Clinics need schedules that reflect working hours and existing bookings. ClinicFlow is intended to bring these administrative tasks into one straightforward workflow. Background research identified healthcare software cost as a design consideration [1], [2].

ClinicFlow is an administrative clinic-finding and appointment system for patients and clinic staff. It does not replace an electronic medical record system. It will not collect clinical notes or provide diagnoses, treatment plans, prescriptions, or medical decision support. The system uses only account and booking information needed for these workflows: an account email managed by Supabase Auth and a booking profile with the patient’s name and optional phone number.

Patients can search active clinics by manually entering a city or postal code, set a maximum distance, apply clinic filters, review services and approximate distance, see the next available appointment time, book, and view upcoming bookings. The search does not use device location. ClinicFlow’s database validates booking requests, prevents overlapping active appointments, and records appointment audit events.

The current prototype is a responsive React, TypeScript, and Vite application connected to Supabase Auth and PostgreSQL. It uses synthetic Ottawa clinic and schedule data, including postal-prefix lookup points K1N and K2G; this demonstrates the search workflow but is not comprehensive Canadian clinic coverage. Patients can manage a minimal profile, search clinics, review availability, book, cancel, reschedule, and view upcoming appointments. Clinic staff with trusted active memberships can manage recurring hours and unavailable periods, view daily schedules, set appointment duration, check patients in, cancel appointments, and record completed or no-show outcomes. The prototype also includes waitlist offers, accessibility controls, and in-app notifications. External email/SMS/push delivery, full French translation, and broader verified clinic-location data remain incomplete.

Capacitor Android and iOS project files are configured to package the existing React/Vite interface. Verification and password-recovery callbacks use a custom app URL scheme. Native compilation, hosted Auth redirect configuration, callback handoff, and real-device testing still need to be verified.

This report presents the detailed design of ClinicFlow’s global concept and subsystems, followed by the bill of materials and preliminary test plan, task planning, and conclusions about the project’s current status and remaining work.

3. # **Detailed Design – Subsystems**

**3.1 Database design**   
Supabase Auth stores and verifies login credentials. After a user registers, a database trigger creates the matching application profile using the Auth user ID. A patient then completes a minimal booking profile containing their name and optional phone number. ClinicFlow does not store passwords, custom authentication tokens, date of birth, medical records, or clinical notes.

PostgreSQL stores profiles, patient profiles, staff profiles, clinic memberships, clinic hours, staff hours, clinics, appointments, waitlist entries, notifications, announcements, accessibility preferences, and audit logs. Row Level Security controls access to these records. Patients can access their own information and bookings. Clinic staff access is limited by active clinic membership. Elevated staff and administrator access must be assigned through a trusted administrative process, not through public signup.

Availability, booking, cancellation, rescheduling, check-in, and appointment outcomes use validated PostgreSQL functions. Database constraints prevent overlapping active appointments. Triggers audit appointment changes and selected patient-profile, membership, schedule, schedule-block, announcement, waitlist, and accessibility-preference changes. Audit records contain actor, action, entity, and time without patient names or contact details. Reads and failed actions are not audited.

**3.4 Calendar and Availability**  
**![][image19]![][image20]**

![][image21]

The Calendar subsystem handles availability and booking appointments at clinics. Clinics can set working times and unavailable times. Only available times are displayed. 

The subsystem has four key parts;

Patient Mobile App

Displays available times for a selected clinic. Allows patients to select an available time to book an appointment. Unavailable times are marked.

Clinic Interface

A way for clinic staff to set working times, length of appointments, unavailable times and see/cancel booked appointments.

Calendar Backend

Generates the list of time slots based on clinic settings. This will show available, booked and unavailable times. When a patient books a time, the backend will check to make sure it is available, this stops multiple patients booking the same time. The backend will find the next available time for each clinic and send this back to the Clinic Finder. All bookings, cancellations and changes will be logged.

Appointment Database

Stores the clinic settings, unavailable times and appointments. Shows which patient booked the appointment.

Data Flow

Clinic staff enter working times and unavailable times in the clinic interface. The backend will store this in the database. When a patient opens up a clinic calendar, the backend will read the clinic settings and appointments to generate the timeslots for that day. Available times are sent back to the app. When a patient selects an available time, the backend will check if the time is available. If it is, the appointment will be stored and confirmation will be given to the patient. The appointment will also be displayed to the clinic. If not available, the patient will be informed the time was unavailable and the list will refresh. The backend will send the next available time back to the Clinic Finder.

Privacy & Security

Only patients can view/change their own appointments. Clinics can only see appointments made with them. Other patients’ appointments will only be displayed as unavailable. No personal information will be given. Information sent to/from the app, backend and database should follow the privacy and security requirements of the project.

Inputs & Outputs

Clinic working times, length of appointment, unavailable times, patient booking/cancelling. List of available times, confirmations of bookings, clinic schedule, next available time to Clinic Finder.
